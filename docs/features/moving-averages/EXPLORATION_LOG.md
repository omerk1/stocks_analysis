## 2026-09-16

- **Wide feature x horizon IC sweep** (`src/signals/moving_averages/feature_sweep.py` — moved 2026-09-18 from `notebooks/track_a_feature_sweep.py`, run via `python -m src.signals.moving_averages.feature_sweep`; committed; regenerates the full 348-row table and the 48-row redundancy-check table under `output/moving_averages/` on rerun — `output/` is gitignored per this repo's own convention, so those two CSVs themselves aren't tracked, only the script that reproduces them). Motivation: not a single hypothesis — a broad scan for candidate model-input features, per the user's own stated goal (feature engineering for a downstream model, not one publishable claim). Grid: `dist_pct`/`dist_atr`/`dist_z` x {sma,ema} x {20,50,150,200} (24), `slope_log_k` x same x k in {5,21,63} (24), `above` boolean state x same (8), `dist_from_52w_high`/`dist_from_52w_low` (2) — 58 features x horizons {5,10,21,42,63,126} = 348 cells. Same U1 universe/window as every other module (408 S&P 500 constituents, 2010-01-01 → 2021-12-31, holdout untouched). Method: mean daily rank-IC per cell (`modules/cross_sectional.py::daily_rank_ic`, reused unchanged), no bootstrap/CI (Track A, cheap screen not confirmation); the top 25 |IC| cells got a richer read (C1 top-decile-vs-rest spread, hit-rate delta, win/loss ratio, skew — `stats/controls.py`/`stats/shape.py`, both reused unchanged). EXPLORATORY, no significance bar, nothing here is a finding.
- **Every cell in the grid is weak in absolute terms** — the largest |mean IC| in the whole 348-cell sweep is 0.038 (`dist_from_52w_low`@126d), and the great majority of the top-25 sit in the 0.018-0.022 range. Consistent with this whole study's running theme (M4/M1/M11/§7.5/M2): this looks like a weak-signal domain across the board, not a "found a strong single predictor" domain. Read the ranking below as relative, not absolute.
- **`dist_from_52w_high`/`dist_from_52w_low` are the clear standouts, and specifically at longer horizons.** `dist_from_52w_low`@126d: mean IC +0.0385 (further above the 52-week low → higher forward return). `dist_from_52w_high`@126d: mean IC -0.0269 (closer to the 52-week high → lower forward return). Both are roughly 2x the next-best cell's magnitude, and both get *stronger*, not weaker, as horizon lengthens from 21d out to 126d — the opposite horizon pattern from the SMA-distance features below, which peak around 21-42d and don't appear in the top 25 past 42d. **Directly corroborates M2's ablation finding** (`FINDINGS.md`/`EXPERIMENTS.csv`, `ablation_tt_c7_h21`): criterion 7 (within 25% of the 52-week high) was M2's single largest attribution coefficient, negative, at 21d — this sweep finds the same feature is the strongest raw-IC cell in the *entire* grid, and gets stronger at longer horizons M2 never tested. Two independent readings (M2's controlled-but-uncontrolled-OLS attribution vs. this sweep's plain rank-IC) landing on the same feature and sign is worth a real look — genuinely the most promising candidate this sweep surfaced, at 63d/126d specifically, not yet tested with any real control (C1/C2) or CI at those horizons.
- **`dist_pct` and `dist_atr` are highly redundant (0.96-0.98 median per-date Spearman) at *every* lookback, both families** — generalizes M4's SMA20-specific finding (`PREREGISTRATION.md`, 2026-09-09 addendum) to the full grid, previously unchecked at SMA50/150/200 (flagged as an open item in `STATUS.md`'s whole-grid FDR section). Don't treat `dist_pct`/`dist_atr` as two separate model features at any lookback — pick one.
- **`dist_z` decorrelates meaningfully from `dist_pct`/`dist_atr` as lookback grows — not a uniform redundancy.** vs. `dist_pct`: 0.94 (SMA20) → 0.89 (SMA50) → 0.74 (SMA150) → 0.69 (SMA200); EMA family similar. So `dist_z` is close to redundant at SMA20 (matches M4's finding) but a genuinely more separate signal at the longer lookbacks — worth keeping as its own feature there, not dropped on the SMA20 precedent.
- **Slope-vs-distance correlation confirms DESIGN §6.0's identity empirically, and shows exactly where it breaks down.** EMA, short k: `dist_pct_ema_200` vs. `slope_log_5_ema_200` = 0.986 — essentially the same variable, as the exact k=1 algebraic identity predicts. SMA, short k: `dist_pct_sma_20` vs. `slope_log_5_sma_20` = 0.71 — same direction, much weaker (SMA slope is "momentum with smoothed endpoints," not the same variable). Both families decorrelate substantially at k=63 (SMA20: 0.18, EMA20: 0.32 — the two lowest in the whole slope-vs-distance table) — `slope_log_63` is the one slope variant that's a genuinely separate signal from raw distance at every lookback/family; `slope_log_5` on an EMA is close to redundant with distance and shouldn't be built as if it were confirming information. Relevant groundwork for M6 if it's picked up, not a new finding on its own (echoes the 2026-09-07 `corr=0.947` sanity check above, now across the full grid instead of one cell).

Motivation: M6.2 (Track B, now closed — see `STATUS.md`) found two real cells using only the top/bottom `dist_atr` decile and a binary slope sign at SMA50; this asks whether that's a genuine, smooth 2D interaction across the *whole* distance range (both sides of the MA, all lookbacks/families this study built) or an artifact of picking the extremes. Grid: `dist_atr` decile (0–9) × `slope_log_21` rising-vs-falling tercile delta (middle tercile dropped from the comparison, matching M6.2's own binary construction), × {sma,ema} × {20,50,150,200} — 8 surfaces × 10 deciles = 80 points. No bootstrap CI (Track A, cheap screen). EXPLORATORY, no significance bar, nothing here is a finding.
- **Two real bugs found and fixed while building this, before anything below was trusted** (full account in the script's own docstring): (1) an initial version computed a row-weighted average instead of the date/stratum-equal-weighted average this study's C1/C2 always uses — caught because it sign-flipped M6.2's own SMA50 top-decile result; (2) after fixing that, a three-way "tercile vs. rest" statistic still didn't match M6.2, because M6.2's own construction is a direct two-group (rising vs. falling) delta, not tercile-vs-rest — fixed by dropping the middle tercile from the comparison, then verified this reproduces M6.2 almost exactly (SMA50, decile 9, `dist_atr`: C1 −0.407%/C2 −0.599% here vs. M6.2's own logged −0.398%/−0.592%) before trusting the other 9 deciles × 7 surfaces.
- **Correlation pre-check, mandatory before reading any result**: `slope_log_21` vs. `dist_atr`-family distance correlation rises sharply with lookback for both families (SMA: 0.34 → 0.59 → 0.77 → 0.80 at 20/50/150/200; EMA: 0.59 → 0.78 → 0.91 → 0.93) — SMA150/200 and especially EMA150/200 have the two axes nearly collinear (M6.0's identity showing through), so a "2D surface" there is expected to degenerate toward a single smeared axis, not independent structure.
- **The generalization does not hold up as a smooth effect anywhere.** SMA20/EMA20 (lowest collinearity) stay flat and noisy around zero across all 10 deciles — no structure. SMA50/EMA50 (where M6.2's real finding lives) oscillate up and down across deciles rather than showing a monotonic trend: decile 9 is negative (directionally consistent with M6.2, smaller magnitude given the tercile/4-column-match differences from M6.2's own construction), but deciles 1–2 are *positive* of similar or larger magnitude, and the middle deciles are mixed. SMA150/200 and EMA150/200 swing wildly (multiple percentage points, e.g. SMA200 decile 2 at +4.4%, EMA200 decile 8 at +4.4%) — consistent with the collinearity warning above, essentially uninterpretable. **Read: M6.2's SMA50 finding sits at one decile surrounded by noise in both directions, not on a gradient — the "lone bright pixel" pattern DESIGN §6.7's plateau rule says to call noise, not a confirmed wider phenomenon.** Consistent with (does not contradict) M6.2's own result already having failed the whole-grid FDR pass on independent grounds.
